# Refine from actual viewing

Adapted from Ryan's supplied `build-your-own-animate-guide.pdf`, especially pages 5–8. The document is reference material; its sample prompts do not authorize installing services, publishing, purchases, or adopting the author's taste.

1. Watch an actual render. Preserve what worked and identify the concrete readability, pacing, placement, or motion issue.
2. Turn Ryan's observation into a pattern: what should happen, when, and why. Ask only when its intended generality is unclear; a one-video change need not become a global rule.
3. Fold the accepted pattern into the existing relevant instruction. Remove superseded wording; avoid a growing dated rules appendix.
4. When the pattern changes visuals or motion, render the same brief and compare. Keep source, trim, transcript and dimensions fixed so the comparison isolates the change. Do not silently call the new result approved.

## Turn feedback into a usable decision

Record the observation with a timecode, the desired behavior, its reason, its scope (this video, this project, or global), and the evidence that would show improvement. Put project-specific choices in the composition's notes; change global skill rules only when that scope is explicit. Preserve what Ryan liked as well as what needs repair.

Translate visual references into concrete palette, typography, spacing, caption placement and motion choices. Mark unreviewed choices as candidates. The guide's examples illustrate how to express taste; they are not evidence of Ryan's taste.

For the next real-recording validation, choose a short excerpt containing speech, a pause and meaningful screen or face content. Check caption onset after the pause, a difficult name or technical term, overlap with important footage, and original-audio synchronization near the start and end. Review readability at the intended viewing size and listen to the encoded result. Record the source excerpt, output path, inspected timecodes, what improved, and unresolved feedback in the project notes. A generic script demo cannot close this check.

Use the stable latest-trial output for routine reruns; preserve an accepted reference and retain an extra comparison version only when requested. Separate technical verification from Ryan's creative acceptance. Stop iterating when the requested change is met or a concrete blocker needs input.

## Current decisions
