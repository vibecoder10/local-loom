# Copy that survives motion

Write the message before animating it. The viewer must understand the frame during ordinary playback, without pausing.

## Working principles

- Give each beat one useful idea: a concrete benefit, a visual proof or an action. Prefer familiar nouns and active verbs. Remove throat-clearing, unsupported superlatives and implementation details the viewer does not need.
- Let the image carry evidence. A headline names the idea; narration explains it; captions make speech accessible. Do not stack the complete script, a competing headline and tiny labels over the same resource screenshot.
- Build hierarchy through scale, placement, spacing and contrast. Give one phrase the strongest emphasis. Design semantic line breaks; keep names and closely related words together.
- Big headline text and hero numbers sit directly on the scene, with no backing shape or black box, because a box behind a headline reads as a slide, not a scene. Every other authored text background uses 80 percent fill opacity with fully opaque text. Subtitle plates are exempt outside full-screen presenter scenes, and native text inside a captured website stays as captured. In full-screen presenter scenes, the footage stays unobscured: no panel, gradient wash or text background, including subtitle plates. Use compact two-line headlines (about 1.08 line height, no extra inter-line margin) and tight CTA spacing.
- Budget a readable hold after the entrance settles and before the exit begins. Test the actual words against narration and watch at normal speed. Complicated diagrams need additional inspection time. Motion should reveal order or emphasize meaning, then get out of the way of reading.
- Keep important text inside the platform's safe area, away from controls, faces and subtitles. Inspect both a full-size frame and a phone-sized thumbnail. Text-fit checks alone cannot prove readability.

House starting points, not industry standards: try a 3-8 word headline with at most one support line; allow roughly 2-3 seconds of settled reading time for a short title, longer for supporting content. Shorten or split copy before shrinking type. These are prompts for review, not rigid word-count tests. Deliberate kinetic typography may use fewer words and faster beats if meaning remains clear.

For captioned speech, show at most two words at once and synchronize groups to the actual speech. Centre the text, use Inter and highlight the currently spoken word by colour, without an underline. Remove the outgoing group before the next appears. Keep placement stable and clear of scene graphics. Use Inter 800 for occasional titles and hero text. Do not apply Netflix delivery timings as universal requirements for animated headlines.

## Research and scope

- [Google's writing highlights](https://developers.google.com/style/highlights) supports conversational language, active voice, second person and accessible writing. We apply these writing principles to video copy; the source is a documentation guide.
- [Adobe's text animation guide](https://www.adobe.com/uk/products/aftereffects/text-animation.html) describes selective letter/phrase animation and controlling the rate of motion with graph editing. Our adaptation is to choose emphasis deliberately and reserve a settled reading interval.
- [School of Motion on contrast and type](https://schoolofmotion.com/blog/4-simple-tips-to-design-with-contrast-and-type) explains how scale, weight, spacing and tonal contrast establish reading order. Group the words by meaning before styling or animating them.
- [IBM's animation techniques](https://www.ibm.com/design/language/animation/tips-and-techniques/) recommends reducing motion complexity as design complexity increases. Use restrained motion while the viewer reads detailed resource captures; reserve expressive moves for simpler frames.
- [Netflix timed-text requirements](https://partnerhelp.netflixstudios.com/hc/en-us/articles/215758617-Timed-Text-Style-Guide-General-Requirements) specifies two lines maximum, semantic breaks and avoiding overlaps for its subtitles. Its 5/6-second minimum and seven-second maximum are Netflix subtitle specifications, not general motion-graphics headline rules.

Sources checked 2026-09-11. The creative working principles above are our synthesis, not claims that one source mandates every house choice.

## Frame review

Before export, show representative screenshots at the requested aspect ratio. Check the largest headline, the densest resource frame, the caption/presenter combination and the final action. Ask what the viewer should remember from each beat. If two messages compete, simplify the frame. Style-reference files inform palette, typography and materials; website navigation and dashboard chrome do not automatically belong in a video.
