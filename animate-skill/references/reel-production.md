# Reels: visual direction and delivery

The frame should communicate one idea while the spoken words remain easy to follow. These defaults cover portrait and landscape presenter reels. A supplied style reference can change the art direction; explicit corrections override older templates.

## Contents

Type and copy; palette and background; element motion; three standard layouts; actual resources and smooth scrolling; images, vectors and real 3D; opening hooks and beat rhythm; reusable element patterns; eight-reel spotlight template; audio, folders and proof; motion references.

## Type and copy

- Use **Inter** (500 body and subtitles, 800 titles) and **Pixelify Sans 700** for occasional bitmap labels or a single emphasised word. Keep only these two typefaces; both live in `~/AgentVault/Projects/animate/sample/assets/` with their OFL licenses. (RoboNuggets used Outfit + Inter 800; Ryan's approved style replaces them.)
- Centre editorial text. Use subtitles and occasional short titles only. No small supporting copy, eyebrow labels, corner stamps, decorative counters, progress bars or watermarks unless requested.
- Ordinary titles are centred Inter 800 text without small adjacent icons. Icon Title is a separate full-screen pattern with a dominant tool icon and large centred title. Quote CTA keywords with double quotation marks, for example `Comment "PLUGINS"` or `Comment "PROMPT"`, including in subtitles when that keyword is spoken.
- Subtitles follow the actual voice, with **at most two words visible at once**. Time each group against the actual audio and colour the currently spoken word in orange. No underline. A dark rounded plate with light text preserves the original highlight treatment outside full-screen presenter scenes; full-screen presenter subtitles have no plate. Remove outgoing words before new ones appear, including during transitions, so the cap holds on every frame.
- At 1080x1920, start around 68-76 px for subtitles and 96-116 px for titles. These are house starting points, not industry standards. Shorten the copy before shrinking it. Native text inside an actual resource page retains its source typography.
- Big headline text and hero numbers sit directly on the scene, with no backing shape or black box, because a box behind a headline reads as a slide, not a scene. Every other authored text background uses 80 percent fill opacity with fully opaque text. Subtitle plates are exempt outside full-screen presenter scenes, and native text inside a captured website stays as captured. In full-screen presenter scenes, the footage stays unobscured: no panel, gradient wash or text background, including subtitle plates. Use compact two-line headlines (about 1.08 line height, no extra inter-line margin) and tight CTA spacing.

## Palette and background

Use Ryan's approved **What We Built** palette (see SKILL.md rule 2): canvas wolf grey `#D9DCDF`, lighter surfaces `#EEF0F2`, text and deepest shading `#161819`, accent orange `#E65D20` used selectively (one emphasis, the currently spoken word, a key relationship), supporting greys `#6D747A` and `#A7ADB2`. Do not invent additional accent colours for variety. Shadows are hard offsets (`20px 20px 0`), never soft drops.

Background: flat wolf grey, optionally a faint static 12 px pixel grid. It is a pure function of nothing (no animation) unless the brief asks for motion; then sample timeline time deterministically and verify two different times. RoboNuggets' getrubric wave dot-matrix background and warm-paper palette (`#f2efe8` / `#ff6b1a`) are their brand and are not used. To reuse a RoboNuggets element, restyle it first (references/restyle.md).

## Element motion

Default new element transitions to **0.4 seconds**, with sharp acceleration at the start and sharp deceleration into the stop. Use a pronounced ease-in-out such as expo.inOut. Add strong motion blur while the element moves, peaking around mid-transition and returning to zero at both settled endpoints. Keep the readable hold separate from the transition. An explicit duration or specialised continuous motion overrides this default. The 3D entrance is an exception: use a 0.5-second Y turn, remove entry blur and use a 0.1-second opacity fade.

## Three standard layouts

1. Split: portrait has animation above and a square presenter plate below; landscape has animation left and presenter right. Recompose for the aspect ratio; never stretch the portrait frame.
2. Full-screen presenter.
3. Full-screen animation. Reserve this for visuals that need the space, including large or complex infographics.

Include face footage in **at least 65% of scenes**, rounding up: 10 scenes need 7 presenter scenes. Count split and full-screen scenes with a sustained face hold. Ordinary infographics sit above the presenter in portrait or to the left in landscape; full-screen diagrams need a readability/complexity reason. Record counts and ratio in `PRESENTER.json`; a designated placeholder satisfies planned coverage when footage is missing, not a claim of actual face footage.

When footage is missing, use a neutral silhouette or empty plate, **not a saved presenter photo**. Full-screen footage fills the canvas with no visible frame, border, corner rounding or glow. Only split uses rounded corners and a soft zero-offset white outer glow, never a dark drop shadow. On a 2560x1440 source, a proven square crop is 820x820 at (804,394), framing head and upper chest; adjust to your own framing. Place it at (64,868), 952x952, radius 64 on 1080x1920 portrait; at (1040,98), 820x820, radius 54 on 1920x1080 landscape. The landscape full-screen crop is (192,204), 2064x1161: x=7.5%, y=14.1667%, width=80.625%, height=80.625%, scaled uniformly to 1920x1080. Preserve this exact 16:9 source crop in HTML; round to even codec dimensions only if required. Save source/destination rectangles, radius and time ranges in `PRESENTER.json`.

## Actual resources and smooth scrolling

Capture the real public resource. Use mobile emulation so the source's fonts are large enough for a phone. Save the URL, resolved URL, response status, capture date, viewport, pixel size, screenshot and original video. Reject error pages even if a previous capture of the same URL succeeded. Dismiss visible cookie, region and sign-in overlays using their actual controls, then inspect the result.

Present resources inside a rounded browser-window mockup. Show the real hostname plus at most its first path segment in the tab, for example `github.com/example`. The display label has at most one slash: omit protocol, deeper paths, query, fragment and trailing slash. Keep the full original and resolved URLs in capture provenance. Never invent a site label. A house entrance is 60-70 px of upward travel, scale 0.94 to 1, opacity 0 to 1, and a strong 24-30 px blur pulse during a 0.4-second expo.inOut transition. Settle fully for reading; continued scrolling within one scene does not repeat the entrance.

Test both capture methods when scrolling looks poor:

- **Live recording:** record while scrolling the real page. Use animation-frame-driven movement at approximately 20-30 CSS px/second, not coarse timer jumps. Match the recording dimensions to the browser viewport; a larger recording canvas can pad a small mobile viewport with black. Keep the original and note any upscale. Inspect the encoded output for frame cadence, sticky-header jumps, loading glitches and decoder discontinuities.
- **Full-page screenshot:** capture the actual mobile-width page, then move the image behind a clipped browser viewport as a pure function of timeline time. This is the preferred method for static, text-heavy resources when the recording is jittery. Use linear movement around 22 CSS px/second, convert CSS distance to the displayed image scale, and clamp to the image's bottom. This provides smooth movement at the final output frame rate. Clearly record that it is a scrolled screenshot; animated page content is frozen in this method.

Some apps scroll an inner container. Inspect the actual scroll area; a full-page screenshot of the document may only capture one screen. Use a tall mobile-width viewport or capture the appropriate content region without recreating the page. Preserve the original page artwork and text. Do not show a fabricated mock site as captured evidence.

## Images, vectors and real 3D

Ryan's rule replaces RoboNuggets' one-generated-image-per-video requirement: draw explanatory art in code (SVG, CSS, canvas pixel art) in the house style, and register it in Graphics. Use paid image generation only with a cost quote and Ryan's yes. Save the actual tool, prompt and output for any generated image, and never present a generated concept as a real screenshot.

Each foreground image and infographic sequence appears only once within a video. Keep an asset-to-scene ledger and reject repeated IDs, duplicate image hashes or the same diagram with cosmetic changes. A crop, recolour or new caption does not make reused artwork new. Continued movement inside one scene is one appearance. Background wallpaper, presenter placeholder and persistent browser/subtitle chrome may persist; they are not repeated story content. Common assets remain reusable across different videos, but compare the whole batch for repeated infographic scaffolds and motion sequences. Change the visual relationship and layout, rather than reskinning the same diagram.

Build distinct SVG silhouettes around the script's actual subjects. Represent an AI tool in an infographic with its faithful logo SVG from Icons; represent an agent with the ROBO agent icon. Reuse these identities across videos while changing roles and semantic evidence. Prefer symmetrical, balanced custom pixel silhouettes when meaning allows, but never distort authentic logos to force symmetry or pixel styling. Privately compare at least five structures, choose the clearest surprising one, critique it and improve it. Do not default to three icons on an arced dotted line. Reveal parts in spoken order, then animate the relationship, assembly or transformation. A static diagram fading in is insufficient. Never use a scanning line sweeping a monitor or fake code as visual filler, including the old "a setup that fits" treatment. Keep purposeful semantic nodes and replace the scan with a relevant state change. Preserve eased motion, blur and crisp holds.

When real 3D is requested, provide actual editable geometry or GLB, with source files and materials. For a requested logo graphic, use the existing beveled logo geometry from 3D objects, not an app-window or laptop substitute. Follow the requested per-batch placement count. A transparent image with a perspective transform is not freely rotating 3D. Use polished materials, controlled roughness, bevels and lighting to produce shine and subtle shimmer. Do not hide the object's shape under bloom. Present 3D objects directly on the background with no enclosing card, viewport frame or decorative box. Real 3D app components use editable beveled app-window geometry and a timeline-driven interface texture with the app's authentic mark and a typed prompt. Preserve app identity, large readable input, source and GLB. They are illustrative app components, never claimed as captured product evidence. Typing follows the entrance and settles for reading within the 3-second appearance. Preview both aspect ratios without stretching.

The default 3D entrance is **one fast 360-degree Y-axis turn** over 0.5 seconds with sharp ease-in-out, **no entry blur** and only a **0.1-second opacity fade**. Follow with a very slow additional drift of **at most 30 degrees**. Cap the drift and avoid endless spinning. Rotation and opacity are functions of scene-local time, so scrubbing and rendering agree. For complex component requests, make three improvement passes: silhouette and geometry; materials and light; motion and final-frame inspection. Save evidence of what changed at each pass.

Presenter reels export at **30 fps**, preserving true 30 fps footage through the final mix. Sample only animation transforms, 3D, scrolling screenshots and backgrounds at **15 fps** with `animationTime = Math.floor(mediaTime * 15) / 15`; never quantize the global media clock, live footage, audio or subtitle timing. Render independent layers and combine at 30 fps when the renderer cannot keep separate clocks. Duplicating a finished 15 fps reel does not restore presenter motion. Inspect moving regions across adjacent output frames: presenter frames must differ while each sampled animation frame holds for two output frames. Animation-only outputs default to 15 fps unless explicitly overridden. Keep polished lighting and typography; no papercraft unless requested. Optional lens flares must avoid subtitles.

## Opening hooks and beat rhythm

Plan variety at batch level: record each opening, layout sequence, infographic structure and motion pattern before building. Choose portrait openings from presenter-led kinetic type, real resource, infographic transformation or native illustration; a generated image can appear later. Vary the structure and rhythm, not just labels and colours. The split-layout junction hook remains one option, using short Inter 800 type, sharp easing and moving blur. Use staged text, assembly, transforms, spring tickers or wipes when they explain the spoken idea, with crisp reading holds. Landscape presenter reels open on full-screen real footage, without hook text. At the first split, keep **one continuously playing video layer**: slide footage right while its uniform scale, source crop, mask rectangle and corner radius morph together over **0.4 seconds with expo.inOut**. Add moving blur, settle crisp, and bring in only the split plate's white outer glow. Never hard-switch crop files, restart footage or crossfade two faces. Reverse the same motion for a return to full-screen. Sample transforms and mask at 15 fps while footage keeps true 30 fps. Landscape brand films also omit opening hook text; they do not require a presenter. Keep faces and subtitles readable.

A **beat means a scene**. Let the script's idea, phrasing and reading time determine boundaries, up to **6 seconds including transitions**, not a fixed cadence. Hold useful evidence long enough to read; staged infographic reveals can develop within one scene. Subtitle highlighting and background motion alone do not reset the scene clock. In source footage, give cuts **0.1 seconds more breathing room**, usually leaving **0.2-0.3 seconds** between clear spoken beats, near 0.25-0.27 where safe. Protect at least 0.1 seconds before and after audible speech at each trim, including outer reel boundaries where slates permit. ASR timestamps are approximate: inspect the waveform and every join, retaining more source or skipping uncertain trims. Complete words and natural breath override the pause target; never speed speech. Save source keep ranges and handles, then remap video, words, scenes and sound cues. Split longer ideas into related visuals; an explicit longer single-scene request overrides the cap.

Write a concrete problem, useful result or curiosity gap that the script pays off. Match the text, spoken opening and visual proof. Remove greetings and setup, avoid invented statistics, unsupported guarantees and fake urgency. Adapt proven structures instead of copying somebody else's results. Working hooks for the two optimization samples: "Fix your AI output." and "Stop prompting from scratch." These are house drafts, not quotes or performance guarantees.

Research: [Sabrina Ramonov's playbook](https://help.blotato.com/tips-and-tricks/sabrina-playbook) emphasizes the hook and coordination of words and visual. [Blotato hook frameworks](https://help.blotato.com/tips-and-tricks/hooks) provides problem/solution, before/after and quick-win structures. The six-second ceiling and junction placement are user choices, not claims from those sources.

Repeated mentions still get a distinct hero composition, never a recycled infographic sequence. ## Eight-reel spotlight template

For a 16:9 reel-wall showcase, place eight 9:16 placeholders in two rows of four. Move one card into the centre, enlarge it while preserving its portrait aspect, hold for a segment, then return it to the exact original slot before featuring the next. Use a shuffled order that visits each card once, such as 1, 3, 7, 2, 8, 4, 6, 5. Keep the export order fixed or seeded so repeated renders agree. Use 0.4-second transitions with sharp ease-in-out and strong moving blur. Dim the grid behind the featured reel and restore it on return. Keep controls outside the rendered composition; separate-file preview controls should use postMessage so local-file browser restrictions do not break playback. Use placeholders until actual footage is requested.

## Audio, folders and proof

Use the supplied music bed at constant raw-source linear gain **0.1955** throughout, with no sidechain ducking or gain automation. This is 0.23 multiplied by 0.85, a 15-percent relative reduction. Apply it once: use 0.1955 on the original or 0.85 on a verified prior 0.23 music stem, never both. Preserve the original and adjusted stem; loop only when needed. Keep narration separate and check clipping and intelligibility. The four house sound effects stay unchanged: Soft click (click_003.wav) for scene cuts at 80 percent of the processed cue; Confirmation chime (confirmation_001.wav) at 0.30 linear gain for power-ups, checkmarks and success; Switch toggle (switch_001.wav) at 1.0 for hero titles; Glass tick (glass_001.wav) at 1.0 in every Comment "KEYWORD" outro, aligned to the spoken keyword. Keep the Soft click processing exactly `volume=2,alimiter=limit=0.8:attack=1:release=20:level=false:latency=true,volume=0.8`; reducing pre-limiter gain is not equivalent. Other sounds stay available for other projects. Apply every stage once and save each source, cue time, scene, purpose and gain. Inspect the final mix at representative cue times with speech and music present; effects must be heard, not merely logged. Keep listening findings and cue evidence. If listening is unavailable, report that limit and do not claim an audibility review. Do not generate replacement music unless requested; original music uses Suno through Kie AI with provenance. An AI guide voice is temporary, not a presenter clone.

Each video owns its assets, voice, music, references and renders. A separate common folder stores reusable masters, with provenance and reuse maps. Local render copies make each video portable. Never embed credentials or private personal media in a shareable skill.

Show representative actual preview screenshots before export when the user asks for feedback first. After approval, carry the full requested batch through rendering, checks and delivery without asking again. Do not automatically open any HTML or HyperFrames Studio tabs. Use hidden/headless validation and return links or requested screenshots. Open a visible Studio tab only when explicitly asked. On Windows, hide background helpers and browser consoles.

Inspect final encoded frames, check dimensions, frame rate, duration, decoding and audio, and verify that all requested outputs exist. Record measured renderer time per video in `PRODUCTION-TIMES.md`. Distinguish renderer time from total production; do not invent untracked design or review time. Count chat or other delivery as complete only after a confirmed response, and retain receipts to prevent duplicate uploads after an uncertain result.

## Motion references

- [GSAP easing](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/Eases/): choose acceleration and deceleration deliberately. Ease-out brings an entering element to rest; ease-in accelerates an exit. The current house default asks for sharp acceleration and deceleration, so use ease-in-out; do not interpret both ends as mathematical ease-in.
- [IBM animation techniques](https://www.ibm.com/design/language/animation/tips-and-techniques/): keep a clear focal point, use controlled directions and reduce motion complexity when the artwork is complex.
- [Reduced motion](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/prefers-reduced-motion): interactive preview pages should honour the viewer's motion preference and offer pause/replay controls. Video renders use the explicitly selected frame rate and treatment.

The specific durations, distances, colours and layouts above are house choices informed by these principles, not universal prescriptions from the sources.
